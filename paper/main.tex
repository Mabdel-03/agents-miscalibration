\title{\textbf{Capacity, Reasoning, Prompting and Coordination in Small\\
Multi-Agent Language Model Systems:\\
A Factorial Sweep of Accuracy and Calibration}}
\author{\code{full\_sweep\_v1}, the \code{agents\_scaling} project}
\date{Compiled \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This report documents a factorial sweep contrasting one-agent and three-agent
language-model systems across four design axes: model capacity (six Qwen3 sizes from
0.6B to 32B parameters), test-time reasoning budget (five rungs from disabled to
unlimited), system-prompt complexity (two levels), and inter-agent context sharing (two
levels), crossed with four coordination topologies, four question-answering benchmarks
and three replication seeds. The design targets a specific estimand that the existing
multi-agent literature does not measure directly: $\dece$, the difference between the
calibration error of a coordinated system's confidence signal and the mean calibration
error of its constituent agents. The analysis population comprises 3{,}201 completed
cells over 1{,}393 distinct configurations, 638{,}629 graded questions in the core view,
858{,}089 item rows and 3{,}952{,}802 agent-round rows in total. Every result the
analysis pipeline produced is reported here in prose, in tables and in figures, together
with an assessment of which findings are novel relative to the cited literature and what
would be required to establish them more firmly.
\end{abstract}
