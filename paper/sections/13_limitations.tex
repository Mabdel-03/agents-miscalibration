\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Coverage and missingness}

The sweep is 91.9 percent complete against its intended manifest, and the missing 8.1
percent is not a random subset. The 32B block at the two largest reasoning budgets holds 32
and 30 design cells against roughly 47 elsewhere, because those cells are the slowest and
most likely to be preempted. Missingness is therefore correlated with the treatment in
exactly the corner where the capacity axis terminates, which is the corner that determines
whether the capacity axis has plateaued.

Missingness was modelled explicitly over the intended grid, and the resulting figure is in
the atlas. Inverse-probability weighting machinery exists in the analysis library but is not
applied to the headline estimates reported here.

\subsection{The 32B context cap}

The largest model is served at 16{,}384 tokens of context against 32{,}768 for the other
five, because 32B weights in bfloat16 leave insufficient key-value cache room on a single
A100-80GB. The effect is measurable: at b8192 the median 32B cell spends 3{,}472 thinking
tokens against 3{,}981 for 14B, and at unlimited it spends 3{,}124 against 4{,}731.

One hundred and six cells in the analysis population carry the flag, and every headline
claim is reported with and without them in Table~\ref{tab:sens-ctxcap}. The consequence that
cannot be removed by sensitivity analysis is that the apparent 14B to 32B plateau is
measured under a ceiling. A non-capped 32B pass, either on larger-memory hardware or with
tensor parallelism across two devices, is the single experiment that would resolve the
terminal point of the capacity axis.

\subsection{The one-against-three contrast is not an agent-count contrast}

Agent count takes two values, and the value three is perfectly confounded with having a
multi-agent topology. Three further asymmetries compound this. Single-agent cells run five
self-consistency samples and multi-agent cells run none. The two communicating topologies
run two rounds and the other two run one. And a three-agent centralized cell has only two
answering sub-agents, so its vote statistics are computed over a different support than a
three-agent voting cell.

The comparison is therefore between system families, not between agent counts, and it is
reported that way throughout. The sibling runs covering two, four, five, six and seven
agents exist as frozen cell manifests but have not been ingested, so no agent-count curve
can be fitted from the present data.

\subsection{Design coverage and confounded manipulations}

Two prompt levels and one context-sharing level were removed by the mid-run trim. The
context variable is therefore effectively binary, and the intermediate condition that would
distinguish sharing scratchpad content from sharing full chains of thought was never
executed post-trim.

The prompt axis confounds length with structure: level 3 is both longer and more structured
than level 0, and a two-level design cannot separate them. Both length and a deterministic
quality composite are recorded per cell, which permits post-hoc adjustment but not
identification.

The reasoning axis confounds thinking depth with sampling temperature, because the
thinking-enabled and thinking-disabled conditions run at 0.6 and 0.7 respectively with
different nucleus thresholds. Part of the observed confidence shift with reasoning may
therefore be a sampling effect.

Prompt level 1 exists in the data through the 360 injected single-agent baselines but is
excluded from the analysis population by the core-grid predicate. Those baselines are still
used for the efficiency metrics, where the join prefers a same-prompt-level baseline and
falls back to any level, so a minority of efficiency values compare across prompt levels.

\subsection{Measurement limitations}

The mathematical benchmark has no option-logprob path, so per-agent and final-producer
calibration are undefined there and 801 of 3{,}201 cells have missing calibration columns by
construction. Every calibration conclusion in this report is a multiple-choice conclusion.

Option letters are shuffled independently per question and seed, which is correct for
avoiding position bias but makes cross-cell wrong-answer collisions unidentifiable. The
voting-lift decomposition cannot be computed as a result, and its null finding should not be
read as evidence against error correlation.

The per-cell bootstrap holds bin and atom assignments fixed across replicates. This is a
standard approximation and is adequate for standard errors, but it understates uncertainty
in cells where the bin edges are themselves unstable.

The efficiency metrics are missing for 96 cells whose matched single-agent baseline had not
completed at ingestion time, concentrated at 32B.

\subsection{Generality}

All results come from a single model family, one instruction-tuned dense Qwen3 lineage.
Holding the family fixed is what makes the capacity axis interpretable, and it is also what
prevents any claim about models in general. A mixture-of-experts checkpoint exists in the
model registry but was not swept.

All four benchmarks are static, English, single-turn question answering with no tool use and
no environment interaction. The coordination structures tested are correspondingly simple.
The centralized topology in particular may be more valuable in tool-routing settings that
this sweep does not represent, which is the setting \citet{wong2026teamofthoughts} address,
so the negative result on centralized orchestration should not be generalised beyond
question answering.

Three seeds per configuration is enough to estimate a mean and not enough to model seed
variance as a random effect, which is why seeds are resampled within design cell as a
bootstrap stage rather than modelled.

\subsection{Provenance}

The cached tables analysed here were built on 2026-07-09 by the ingest pipeline that was
current at that time. The repository has since adopted a stricter homogeneous-protocol
contract under which this run is classified as supplementary mixed-protocol evidence rather
than as the authoritative dataset, and a re-run under that contract has not produced data.
The results in this report are therefore the best available evidence for this design, and
they are not the frozen release-grade numbers that the newer contract is intended to
produce. Appendix~\ref{app:provenance} records the exact inputs, hashes and versions.

\subsection{Follow-up experiments in priority order}

The following are ordered by how much they would change the conclusions relative to their
cost.

Ingest the existing five- and seven-agent manifests. This tests whether the capacity effect
on $\dvote$ survives a finer vote support, which is the leading alternative explanation for
the report's second-most-important finding, and the runs are already specified.

Add a one-round decentralized condition. This separates communication from generation
compute in every topology contrast, and is a small addition to the existing grid.

Log canonical answer identities alongside positional option letters. This costs nothing at
run time and makes the voting-lift decomposition computable.

Run a non-capped 32B pass. This resolves the terminal point of the capacity axis and removes
the confound from the matched-compute comparison.

Fit a held-out confidence combiner over the vote fraction, producer probability, agreement
level and verbalized signal. This bounds how much of the agreement-calibration failure is
recoverable by a better aggregation rule.

Vary option-set cardinality within a fixed benchmark. This tests the proposed mechanism for
the $\dvote$ sign reversal directly.

Compare compressed rationales against raw chains of thought. This separates the information
transfer from the convergence effect in the context-sharing axis.

Complete the remaining intended cells, prioritising the 32B long-reasoning corner and the
outstanding single-agent baselines, before any configuration-level ranking claim is made.
