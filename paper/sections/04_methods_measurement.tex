\subsection{Confidence signals}

Table~\ref{tab:signals} lists the confidence signals measured.

\intable{tab_signals}

Three elicitation mechanisms produce them.

\textbf{Option logprobs.} A separate completion-style request scores the option letters at
temperature zero with a single generated token and a twenty-logprob cap. Token matching
strips leading whitespace so that both spaced and unspaced letter tokens map to the same
option. Letters absent from the returned top-$k$ receive negative infinity, and the
resulting probabilities are renormalised over the option set. If no option appears in the
top-$k$ at all, the distribution is uniform, which is detectable downstream as equal mass.
This mechanism does not exist for free-form answers, so the mathematical benchmark has no
option-logprob confidence.

\textbf{Verbalized confidence.} A case-insensitive pattern extracts a percentage from the
response, taking the last match, dividing by one hundred and clipping to the unit
interval. This is elicited by the answer-format instruction appended to every question.

\textbf{Self-consistency.} Single-agent cells draw five samples and derive both a modal
answer share, in the manner of \citet{wang2023selfconsistency}, and a semantic-entropy
confidence based on clustering semantically equivalent answers \citep{kuhn2023semantic}. Multi-agent cells do not run self-consistency,
which is one of the structural asymmetries in the one-against-three contrast.

The system-level aggregate exposes up to seven derived quantities per question, of which
the report uses three: the vote fraction, the final producer's own option-logprob
confidence, and the mean option-logprob confidence over the agreeing side. The definition
of ``producer'' differs by topology: for voting topologies it is the winning-side agent
with the highest probability on the final answer; for centralized cells the orchestrator
is substituted.

One degeneracy is handled explicitly. On the mathematical benchmark the final-producer
logprob is recorded as identically zero because there is no option-logprob path for
free-form answers. Treating that as a confidence signal would make its calibration error
numerically equal to the accuracy, which is meaningless. The pipeline therefore sets the
signal to missing whenever its maximum is non-positive, both when computing the point
estimate and when bootstrapping it.

\subsection{Calibration estimators}

Table~\ref{tab:estimators} defines every estimator computed.

\intable{tab_estimators}

The choice of estimator per signal is fixed in advance by the signal's support rather than
selected after inspecting results. Continuous signals use an equal-mass ten-bin
$\ell_1$ estimator, whose bin edges are sample quantiles with infinite endpoints and
deduplication. The vote fraction is discrete: with three agents its support is
$\{1/3, 2/3, 1\}$ and with two answering sub-agents it is $\{1/2, 1\}$, so binning it is
not meaningful and it is scored by exact atom, grouping on distinct rounded values.

Three robustness estimators accompany the primary one. The debiased $\ell_2$ estimator of
\citet{kumar2019verified} subtracts the within-bin sampling variance and is reported for
continuous signals only, being undefined for a discrete support. The Brier decomposition
\citep{murphy1973} separates reliability from resolution and base-rate uncertainty. The
fifteen-bin equal-width plug-in estimator is computed and reported once, purely for
comparability with the calibration literature that uses it \citep{guo2017calibration};
it is not the primary estimator because it is biased upward at small sample sizes, an
effect the sweep exhibits directly and which Section~\ref{sec:health-estimators}
quantifies.

Two diagnostics that are not calibration errors are also computed. The Cox recalibration
fit \citep{cox1958} regresses correctness on the logit of confidence and reports an
intercept and a slope, which separate systematic bias from insufficient or excessive
sharpness. The area under the receiver operating characteristic separates discrimination
from calibration, distinguishing a signal that ranks correct answers well but is
miscalibrated from one that does neither.

\subsection{The headline estimand}

Two parallel families of columns are produced. One uses the fifteen-bin plug-in estimator
for all three signals, which preserves parity with the package-native implementation and
provides the comparability numbers. The other uses the pre-registered estimator per
signal, and is primary.

In the primary family the two deltas are

\begin{align}
\dvote &= \mathrm{ECE}_{\mathrm{atom}}\bigl(c_{\mathrm{vote}}\bigr) - \mathrm{ECE}_{10}\bigl(c_{\mathrm{agent}}\bigr), \\
\dfp &= \mathrm{ECE}_{10}\bigl(c_{\mathrm{fp}}\bigr) - \mathrm{ECE}_{10}\bigl(c_{\mathrm{agent}}\bigr).
\end{align}

The estimators are deliberately mixed within $\dvote$, each signal being scored by the
estimator appropriate to its support. This is a considered choice rather than an
oversight: forcing the discrete vote fraction through a ten-bin quantile estimator would
produce bins that are either empty or identical to the atoms, and forcing the continuous
per-agent signal through an atom estimator would give one group per distinct floating-point
value. The plug-in family is reported alongside precisely so that a reader who prefers a
single estimator throughout can see what difference it makes; Section~\ref{sec:health-estimators}
shows the two families agree to machine precision on the parity columns.

The per-agent baseline pools every agent-round in the cell, taking as confidence the
option-logprob probability of the answer that agent chose and as outcome whether that
agent was individually correct. This is the mean calibration error of the parts, and it is
what a coordinated system must beat to be said to improve calibration.

\subsection{Efficiency metrics}
\label{sec:efficiency-defs}

Three coordination metrics follow the framework of \citet{kim2025scaling}, computed
against a matched single-agent baseline:

\begin{align}
E_c &= \frac{\mathrm{success\ rate}}{\mathrm{turns}\,/\,\mathrm{turns}_{\mathrm{SAS}}},
&A_e &= \frac{\mathrm{error\ rate}}{\mathrm{error\ rate}_{\mathrm{SAS}}},
&O\% &= 100 \times \frac{\mathrm{turns} - \mathrm{turns}_{\mathrm{SAS}}}{\mathrm{turns}_{\mathrm{SAS}}}.
\end{align}

Coordination efficiency $E_c$ penalises accuracy by the relative number of generation
turns. Error amplification $A_e$ exceeds one when the system makes more errors than a
single agent of the same capacity. Turn overhead $O\%$ is the additional generation cost
as a percentage. Message density and cross-agent output similarity are also computed but
are not central to the results reported here.

The baseline join is keyed on capacity, benchmark, seed and reasoning rung. Where a
single-agent cell exists at the same prompt level it is preferred; otherwise any prompt
level is accepted. This differs from the package-native implementation, which keys only on
the four factors and therefore silently mixes prompt levels for the many keys that have
single-agent cells at several prompt levels. All three metrics are set to missing when
either the multi-agent cell or its baseline contains any censored question.

A separate cost proxy, the product of parameter count and mean total generated tokens, is
used for the compute-frontier analysis. Because total tokens are summed over agents and
rounds, a multi-agent cell automatically carries three to six times the cost of a
single-agent cell at the same capacity, which is the intended behaviour for a
matched-compute comparison.

\subsection{Uncertainty}

Two bootstrap schemes operate at different levels.

\textbf{Per-cell standard errors.} Within a cell, 500 replicates resample the cell's
questions by multinomial weights. Bin and atom assignments are computed once on the
observed sample and held fixed across replicates, which reduces the calculation to a
reweighting and makes it feasible across several thousand cells; treating bin edges as
fixed is a standard approximation and is adequate for standard errors. One question
resample is shared across all three signals within a replicate, so the delta standard
errors are properly paired and are smaller than the sum of the component errors. Per-agent
rows inherit the weight of their parent question. A cell with fewer than ten usable
calibration points, or any censored question, receives missing standard errors rather than
an unstable estimate.

\textbf{Across-cell intervals.} Every marginal mean and matched contrast in this report
uses a two-stage hierarchical bootstrap. The first stage resamples question identifiers
once per replicate and applies that single resample jointly to every configuration, which
is necessary because all configurations within a benchmark answer the same question set
and are therefore correlated through question difficulty. The second stage resamples seeds
with replacement within each design cell, grouping cells by their observed seed
multiplicity. Pooling methods that assume independence across configurations are not valid
here and are not used.

Matched contrasts are computed on pairs of cells that agree on every factor other than the
contrasted one and on seed, averaged at the matched-design level, and then bootstrapped.
This makes them within-design differences rather than marginal comparisons, which matters
because coverage is not uniform across the grid.

The stratified contrasts recompute the same quantity separately inside each combination of
benchmark, capacity and reasoning rung. These are the appropriate read whenever a factor
could plausibly interact with capacity or context pressure. Many stratified cells rest on
few matched pairs, and the minimum pair count is reported alongside every stratified
estimate so that thin strata are visible.

Item-level models use a logistic specification with question fixed effects absorbed and
standard errors clustered by design cell, dropping questions answered correctly or
incorrectly by every configuration since they carry no within-question information.
Cell-level calibration outcomes, which are bounded in the unit interval, use a fractional
logit. Where multiple hypotheses are tested across strata, the Benjamini-Hochberg
procedure controls the false discovery rate.

\subsection{Ingestion and the analysis population}

Raw per-question streams are deduplicated by question identifier within a cell, keeping
the first occurrence. Malformed lines are counted and skipped. A cell enters the canonical
cell table only if it is semantically complete; partial progress is never promoted to a
complete-cell estimate.

The analysis population for this report is the post-trim core view: complete cells whose
prompt level is 0 or 3 and whose context level is artifact-only or plus-CoT. This
predicate is factor-level membership, not a calendar filter. Context-capped 32B cells are
retained and flagged rather than dropped, so that every claim involving them can be
reported with and without.
