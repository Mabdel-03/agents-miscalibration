\section{Data health and the analysis population}
\label{sec:health}

\subsection{Attrition}

Table~\ref{tab:attrition} traces the funnel from raw output directories to the analysis
population, and Table~\ref{tab:attrition-breakdown} decomposes the loss by factor level.

\intable{tab_attrition}

Of 4{,}680 intended cells, 4{,}301 are semantically complete, a completion rate of 91.9
percent. The 5{,}191 directories present on disk exceed the intended count because the
directory set includes partial work, pre-trim configurations orphaned by the grid change,
and directories touched but not finished; the directory count is therefore not a
denominator for completion.

\intable{tab_attrition_breakdown}

The 1{,}100 cells that are complete but excluded from the analysis population are not a
random subset. Prompt level 1 contributes 573 of them and prompt level 2 contributes 347,
which together account for 84 percent of the loss. These are the levels the trim removed,
plus the injected single-agent baseline cells that the sweep still generates at prompt
level 1 and that the core-grid predicate excludes. A further 376 exclusions carry the
\code{plus\_intermediate} context level, which the trim also removed. The exclusion is
therefore almost entirely a consequence of the design change described in
Section~\ref{sec:trim} rather than of execution failure.

The analysis population is 3{,}201 cells over 1{,}393 distinct design cells, comprising
638{,}629 graded questions. The full item table holds 858{,}089 rows and the agent-round
table holds 3{,}952{,}802 rows, both counted before the core-grid filter.

\subsection{Duplicate rows and malformed output}

The resumable dispatcher can append a question's result more than once when a task is
preempted after writing but before recording progress. This occurred: 41{,}918 duplicate
question rows were dropped across 399 cells during ingestion, keeping the first occurrence
of each question identifier. Before deduplication the affected cells reported inflated
question counts, in the worst case more than twice the intended 200.

The correction is not cosmetic. Across affected cells, deduplication shifts the reported
accuracy by a mean absolute 0.0114 and a maximum of 0.0677. The larger figure is
comparable to several of the treatment effects reported in this document, so any analysis
built on the raw streams rather than the deduplicated table would be materially wrong for
those cells. After deduplication, no cell has an unexpected question count.

A further 506 malformed output lines were skipped, and two cells were unrecoverable. Both
counts are consistent with tasks being killed mid-write on a preemptable partition.

\subsection{Seed replication}

Table~\ref{tab:seed-replication} reports the across-seed dispersion of accuracy within
design cells, and Figure~\ref{fig:seed-funnel} plots it against the binomial expectation
implied by question sampling alone.

\intable{tab_seed_replication}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{fig_seed_funnel.pdf}
\caption{Across-seed accuracy standard deviation against design-cell mean accuracy, with
the binomial curve for a 200-question sample overlaid. Points above the curve indicate
variance beyond question sampling.}
\label{fig:seed-funnel}
\end{figure}

Seed dispersion is of the same order as the binomial expectation, which means replication
noise is dominated by which questions were asked rather than by sampling temperature or
option-position effects. This supports the decision to resample questions jointly across
configurations in the first bootstrap stage: the shared question set is the dominant
correlation channel between cells.

\subsection{Estimator agreement}
\label{sec:health-estimators}

The pipeline computes two families of calibration columns, one matching the package-native
fifteen-bin plug-in estimator and one using the pre-registered estimator per signal. On
the parity columns the two agree to $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$, that is to floating-point
precision, confirming that the recomputation is faithful and that any difference between
the families is attributable to the estimator choice rather than to an implementation
discrepancy.

Table~\ref{tab:estimator-comparison} reports every calibration estimator, averaged over
multiple-choice cells, for each of the three signals.

\intable{tab_estimator_comparison}

Two properties of the estimators are visible directly in the sweep and justify the
pre-registration. The plug-in estimator inflates at small sample sizes while the debiased
estimator stays flat, so a comparison between conditions with unequal usable sample sizes
would be biased if it used the plug-in estimator. And the vote-fraction signal has support
on three atoms only, so a ten-bin quantile estimator applied to it would produce bins that
either duplicate the atoms or are empty. The atom estimator is the only well-defined
choice for that signal.

Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap-ses} reports the distribution of per-cell bootstrap standard
errors.

\intable{tab_bootstrap_ses}

The paired construction matters: because one question resample is shared across all three
signals within a replicate, the delta standard errors are substantially smaller than the
sum of the component standard errors would suggest.

\subsection{Missing calibration by construction}

Calibration columns are missing for 801 of the 3{,}201 cells. This is not attrition. The
mathematical benchmark has no option-logprob path, so per-agent and final-producer
calibration are undefined there by construction, and the final-producer signal is
additionally guarded against its degenerate all-zero form. The vote-fraction column has no
missing values, because agreement is measurable regardless of answer format, which is why
the mathematical benchmark still contributes to the agreement-calibration results.

The three efficiency metrics are missing for 96 cells. Every one of these is a multi-agent
cell whose matched single-agent baseline had not completed at the time of ingestion, and
the missing baselines are concentrated at 32B. No join defect was found.

\subsection{The 32B context cap}
\label{sec:health-cap}

The 32B model is served at half the context length of the other five. The consequence is
observable in the realized token counts rather than in a served error: at b8192 the median
realized reasoning token count is 3{,}472 for 32B against 3{,}981 for 14B, and at
unlimited it is 3{,}124 against 4{,}731. The larger model spends fewer thinking tokens than
the smaller one at the same nominal budget, which is the signature of a ceiling rather than
of a modelling choice.

One hundred and six cells in the analysis population carry the context-cap flag.
Table~\ref{tab:sens-ctxcap} repeats the headline outcome means with and without them.

\intable{tab_sens_ctxcap}

Every claim in this report that involves the 32B endpoint at b8192 or unlimited should be
read as capacity-limited. In particular, the apparent plateau between 14B and 32B reported
in Section~\ref{sec:scaling} cannot be separated from the cap with the present data.

\subsection{Execution-era sensitivity}

Because the grid was trimmed mid-run, 542 of the 3{,}201 analysed cells started before the
trim commit epoch and 2{,}659 after. Table~\ref{tab:sens-era} compares outcomes across the
two eras.

\intable{tab_sens_era}

The comparison is a check for serving-stack drift rather than a treatment contrast: the
two eras differ in when cells ran, not in what they ran, since factor-level membership is
enforced separately. No systematic era effect large enough to reinterpret any result
appears.

Appendix~\ref{app:axes} reproduces the complete column glossary for the analysis

\begin{figure}[H]

The structure is informative for reading the rest of the report. Within a signal, the
alternative estimators are close to collinear, which is why the robustness columns rarely
change a conclusion. Across signals, the per-agent, vote and final-producer families are
substantially independent, which is why separating them in Equation~\ref{eq:estimand} is
necessary rather than pedantic.
